[ Case study (03) ] — UX Project · Capstone

Guide App

A learning-management platform reimagined around byte-sized stories. I led UX research and product design to reduce course editing time by 30% — without forcing creators into external tools.

Role
UI/UX Designer & Developer
Tools
Figma, Illustrator, FigJam
Team
6-person capstone
Methods
Design Sprint, Usability Testing

Project overview

Guide is a learning-management platform designed to redefine employee training through byte-sized stories. With features that increase engagement, reduce onboarding time, and scale training, Guide aims to become a go-to solution for businesses improving their training processes.

Challenge

Guide struggled to support users managing and interacting with long-form content. Closing this gap was vital to improving the experience and boosting productivity.

Opportunity

The core opportunity: dramatically improve the experience for both content creators and learners within the app.

The goal

Reduce course creation time by 30%

— without sending creators to external editing tools.

Process — Design Sprint

The Design Sprint methodology guided our development. This proven framework let us rapidly address user challenges, generate solutions, and validate them through user-centric testing.

01

Map — aligning goals & discovering problems

Identifying problems

Research showed a major driver of long editing times was the lack of integrated video editing inside Guide. Because of video-length restrictions, users relied on external software before uploading — adding an extra step to every piece of content.

Lean UX & Business Model Canvas

We used Lean UX and the Business Model Canvas to keep findings and solutions aligned with business goals — continuously validating that proposed work addressed user needs while supporting revenue, partnerships, and overall viability.

95%

showed strong interest in micro-learning formats.

80%

found locating the right video to edit most time-consuming.

70%

wanted easier performance tracking and timely feedback.

Competitive analysis

Analysis revealed clear trends in modern LMS products: a strong emphasis on mobile-first design, the rise of short-form video, and seamless integration with workplace tools. Advanced video editing — including AI-powered enhancements — and flexible sharing are increasingly crucial for engaging learners.

02

Sketch — ideation & exploration

Through collaborative sessions — "Crazy 8s" and "How Might We" — we generated a diverse range of concepts.

Persona & user journey

Building personas and mapping journeys gave us insight into how users interact with the product and surfaced the key pain points and opportunities for improvement.

03

Decide — prioritization & storyboarding

Votes & storyboard

To select the most promising solution we ran a structured evaluation: voting on sketches that best met user needs, building a storyboard of the chosen flow, and assessing technical and usability risks with mitigation plans.

04

Prototype — building a realistic model

As UI designer, I led a functional prototype of the "Mark & Snip" tool — letting users tag key sections of long content, create rich snippets, and interact through intuitive markers.

Challenges

Software limits

Prototyping complex interactions — drag-and-drop, resizing — was difficult.

Before & after editing

Keeping the same screen consistent across editing states was tricky throughout the prototype.

05

Validate — user testing & feedback

Using Useberry, I ran usability tests across 6 scenarios with a diverse group — timing performance and following up with interviews, then iterating on the design to resolve issues.

Feedback

Order of the side menu

Users expect editing at the top and settings at the bottom, since editing is used more often.

Confusion on the effect menu

The fade-in animation was expected under "Effect," but the button was unclickable.

Naming confusion

It was hard to relate "markers" to "chapters" (editor-facing vs. viewer-facing).

Iteration

Reorder the side menu

Kept the navigation pattern but resequenced features, with further testing planned.

Add tooltips & coach marks

Introduced first-run coach marks explaining what "Mark" does and how it works.

Add visual cues

Added labels so users see that uploaded videos appear in the timeline.

A plot twist

The client changed direction

Guide pivoted from a Learning Management System to a post-therapy mental-healthcare app, aiming to find a niche market.

06

The detour — adapting to a new direction

Challenges

Research misalignment

We needed fresh research to understand a new target audience and realign our work.

Niche identification

We ran a market analysis of mental-healthcare apps to validate and differentiate Guide.

Restarting the research

1 · Competitive analysis

Compared similar apps' features and business models.

2 · Thematic analysis

Analyzed forum discussions to find patterns in therapists' post-session experiences.

3 · Secondary research

Studied why platforms build native in-app editing despite existing alternatives.

Competitive analysis

I led the competitive analysis of mental-health apps, identifying two categories: therapist-facing and patient-facing. A niche emerged: therapist-focused platforms typically lack the rich multimedia (videos, articles, guided meditations) common in patient apps. Bringing that content to therapist tools could lift patient engagement and create a competitive advantage.

Thematic analysis

Post-session helps — but flexibility is key

Some therapists use structured tracking; others adapt to the client's pace.

Visual progress motivates

Patients stay motivated with visible progress and practical tools.

07

Card sorting — redesigning the navigation

After the first usability test surfaced concerns about navigation categories, we ran card sorting to let people categorize features themselves.

Key insights

Removed 2 categories

"Settings" and "Brand Assets" folded into existing groups to cut cognitive load.

Renamed for clarity

"Caption" became the more direct "Text."

"Chapter" confusion

20% placed it under "Editing," 30% were confused, 50% put it in "Markers."

08

Iteration & cross-platform design

Information architecture

Organized features into a clear IA based on research, testing, and user flows.

Design system

Implemented the client's spacing guidelines and icons.

Component library

Established shared components and design tokens for consistency.

Cross-platform

Consistency across devices

Aligned interactions across devices to reduce the learning curve.

Device-optimized interactions

Tailored platform-specific behaviors where each device behaves differently.

09

Usability test & iteration

We ran scenario-based testing on the mobile prototype with Useberry, covering a full workflow: upload, trim, add text and audio, publish, add to a playlist, and assign to patients.

Feedback

Confusion on chapters

3 of 5 users were unsure how chapters relate to video structure.

Tag creation confusion

Some didn't realize a new tag could be created by typing in the search bar.

Needs more affordance

The upload icon wasn't intuitive enough.

Iteration

Add coach marks

First-run guidance for users new to video editing.

Improve clarity

Updated the search button to read "Create new tags."

Label the upload button

Added descriptive "Upload" text beside the button.

11

Reflection

This ongoing project gave me invaluable experience across the full UX process — from research to prototyping and testing. The Design Sprint proved an effective way to rapidly develop and validate solutions, and building "Mark & Snip" was especially rewarding since it addressed a real pain point. A key challenge was balancing advanced features with a simple, intuitive interface.

Next, I'd invest more time in user testing for more iterative improvement, and explore more robust prototyping tools to simulate complex interactions. Above all, this project reinforced the value of collaboration and communication — translating user needs into tangible solutions and advocating for the user throughout.
