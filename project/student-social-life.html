User Research · University of Maryland

Student Social Life

What encourages — or stops — students from joining campus events and organizations? A user-research study into the social lives of undergraduate Information Science students at UMD.

Role

Interviewer, Moderator, Designer

Tools

Figma, Miro, Interviews & Surveys

Team

6-member group project

Type

User Research

Project overview

Conducted in a User Research course, this project investigated the social lives of undergraduate Information Science students at the University of Maryland.

Through surveys, interviews, and analysis of student experiences, we set out to understand what influences participation in university events and clubs — then turned those insights into a conceptual design for an events platform that better fosters community.

01

Research goals & questions

We wanted to understand the emotions and experiences of students — both on and off campus — around joining events and clubs.

"What encourages or stops students from joining events and organizations? And how do they decide to participate?"

03

Outcomes & core findings

Across the affinity diagram, decision-point model, and personas, several core findings shaped our conceptual design.

Limited awareness

Students often don't know about the many events and clubs on campus.

Lack of relevant information

It's hard to find the when, where, and what of events.

Desire for career-oriented events

Students want more networking events and workshops that help their careers.

Lack of interest

Existing offerings don't always match what students care about.

Not engaging enough

Students prefer fun, active participation over passive listening.

Lack of time & energy

Classes, work, and commitments make it hard to find time for events.

Affinity diagram

The affinity diagram organized interpretation-session notes into a hierarchy that revealed common issues and themes — showing, in one place, the worries and key elements of students' lives relevant to our focus.

Decision-point model

We landed on six main factors: timing, location, relationships, takeaways, interest, and communication.

Personas & journey maps

From our interviews and affinity map, we built two personas representing our users' goals and pain points — a great way to communicate who the target users are and help the client connect with the findings.

04

Wall walk & visioning

Wall walk

We displayed ideas, sketches, and findings on a wall, then moved around to observe, discuss, and annotate. This visual, collaborative approach sparked diverse perspectives and unexpected connections — ideal for finding patterns and refining concepts.

Hot ideas

The wall walk surfaced ideas like tiered events for different skill levels, a customizable app for discovery and friend tracking, and a streamlined platform for easy registration. To fit busy schedules, larger events could run in two sessions — and iSchool-hosted fairs and project showcases could build professional connections.

05

Product concept & lo-fi prototype

Integration with ELMS

Tie the events platform into the university LMS to boost awareness and engagement.

Level assessment

Ease anxiety about skill level in coding events with a clear, accessible assessment.

Schedule planning tool

Help students — especially commuters — navigate scheduling conflicts.

Post-event feedback

A streamlined way to collect feedback and keep improving future events.

06

Reflection

This project was valuable experience in the user-centered design process, from research to conceptual design. Personas and journey maps were especially helpful for understanding our users' diverse needs, and the wall walk fostered real creativity and collaboration. One challenge was balancing a comprehensive platform with simplicity.

Next, I'd user-test the lo-fi prototype to gather usability feedback and explore gamification to incentivize participation. Most of all, this project deepened my ability to synthesize large amounts of qualitative data into actionable insights — and translate them into tangible design — which fuels my passion for user-centered products.
